Getting a driving licence in Kenya is not hard. It just looks hard because the steps are spread across a website, a driving school and a test centre. This is the clean version, in the order it happens. DriveRush is not affiliated with the NTSA, so check the linked NTSA service pages for anything that has changed since this was written.

All NTSA services now run through [eCitizen](https://ecitizen.go.ke): log in there and open the NTSA service portal. The old TIMS system is gone, so ignore any older advice that sends you there.

## The five steps, in order

1. **Enrol at a licensed driving school.** NTSA says the learner is required to enrol into a licensed driving school, and you must be 18 or over. A legitimate school is registered and licensed by NTSA and displays its licence on the premises. More in our [guide to choosing a driving school](/driving-schools-kenya).
2. **Apply for the Provisional Driving Licence (PDL).** On eCitizen, in the NTSA service portal. The fee is KES 650 and the PDL is valid for 3 months. Your L plates must be visible from at least 20 metres, front and rear. Not sure which class you need? We wrote a piece on [picking your licence class](/blogs/class-b-c-d-licence-kenya).
3. **Book the driver test.** You can book after your PDL has matured for 21 days. The fee is KES 1,050. Choose a date, go to the test centre you chose, and sit the test. Your driving school judges when you are ready and helps with booking. If you do not pass, apply for a retest.
4. **Apply for the interim licence.** Once you pass, apply for the Interim Driving Licence (KES 750). It is valid for 6 months and you print it yourself.
5. **Apply for the Smart DL.** The Smart DL costs KES 3,050 and takes 5 working days. You collect the card at a Huduma centre or an NTSA office after you get an SMS.

## What it costs

Official NTSA fees for one licence add up to KES 5,500: PDL 650, driver test 1,050, interim licence 750 and Smart DL 3,050. That is before driving school fees and any retest. Our [cost breakdown](/blogs/cost-of-learning-to-drive-nairobi) explains what else to budget for.

Official sources: [PDL](https://ntsa.go.ke/services/service/provisional-driving-licence-pdl), [driver test booking](https://ntsa.go.ke/services/service/driver-test-booking), [interim licence](https://ntsa.go.ke/services/service/interim-driving-license-idl) and [Smart DL](https://ntsa.go.ke/services/service/application-for-a-smart-driving-licence).

## What the test covers

According to Kenyans.co.ke (2 August 2025), the test covers the Model Town Board and the Highway Code. Road signs are a big part of the Highway Code, so [the signs](/road-signs) are a good place to start. See also our [NTSA test guide](/ntsa-test) and the [Model Town Board guide](/model-town-board). The interactive Model Town Board trainer is not ready yet.

For the driving itself, ask your instructor what to expect on test day. Calm observations, hands at quarter-to-three (or close to it), and a stop that actually stops are good habits whatever the format. Practise the manoeuvres your school teaches somewhere quiet before test day.

## After the card

The day you get your Smart DL is not the day you become a driver. Kenyan roads have rules that are not in the Highway Code, and the first time you take a roundabout at Westlands at 6pm, you'll feel it. We started a guide to that part of driving too: [the unwritten rules of Kenyan roads](/blogs/boda-bodas-matatus-unwritten-rules), and a [first-timer's piece on Thika Road](/blogs/first-timers-guide-thika-road) for when you're ready to be alone in the car.

Until then: pass the test. Then learn to drive. Those are two different sentences.

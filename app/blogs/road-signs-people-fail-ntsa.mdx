Road signs are part of the Highway Code, which the test covers according to Kenyans.co.ke (2 August 2025). We have no data on which signs people get wrong on the day, so this is not a ranking. It's ten pairs from the chart that look alike and mean different things, which is where recall slips under pressure. DriveRush is not affiliated with the NTSA.

Most of these mistakes aren't about not knowing a sign. They're about reading the right sign backwards, or reading the picture and skipping the colour.

## 1. Give way and Stop

[Give way](/road-signs/give-way) is a triangle standing on its point. [Stop](/road-signs/stop) is a red octagon. Both shapes are used for nothing else, so you can tell them apart from far off.

**Give way** means let traffic on the main road go first. If the road is clear, you don't have to come to a full stop. **Stop** means come to a complete stop every time, even if the road looks empty, then go when it's safe.

**Tell them apart:** point down, slow and yield. Eight sides, stop dead.

## 2. Bend left and bend right

[Bend left](/road-signs/bend-left) and [bend right](/road-signs/bend-right) are mirror images: a black line rising from the bottom of the triangle and kinking over near the top.

**Tell them apart:** read the top of the line like a finger. It points the way the road goes.

## 3. One bend or a series

[Successive bends to the left](/road-signs/successive-bends-to-the-left) has a zigzag line with more than one turn. A single bend sign has one kink.

**Tell them apart:** count the turns in the line. A zigzag means a run of curves, and the first step of the zigzag shows the first bend. Don't speed up after the first one.

## 4. Steep descent and steep ascent

[Steep descent](/road-signs/steep-descent-ahead) shows a slope falling from left to right with a small car pointing nose-down. [Steep ascent](/road-signs/steep-ascent-ahead) is the reverse: the slope rises and the car points nose-up.

**Tell them apart:** look at the car's nose. Down means a descent, where you want a low gear and engine braking. Up means a climb.

## 5. Bump and dip

[Severe bump](/road-signs/severe-bump-ahead) shows a single hump rising out of a flat bar. [Severe dip](/road-signs/severe-dip-ahead) shows the bar scooped out in a downward curve. [Uneven road](/road-signs/uneven-road-surface-ahead) has two humps.

**Tell them apart:** up is a bump, down is a dip, two humps is a rough stretch that goes on. All three mean slow down before you reach them, not on top of them.

## 6. Pedestrian crossing and children crossing

[Pedestrian crossing](/road-signs/pedestrian-crossing) shows one adult walking over stripes, and warns of a marked crossing. [Children crossing](/road-signs/children-crossing-ahead) shows two children holding hands and no stripes.

**Tell them apart:** stripes mean one place to watch. No stripes and children mean they may cross anywhere along the stretch. More on this in our guide to [school and children signs](/blogs/school-and-children-signs-kenya).

## 7. Rail crossing with and without a barrier

[Rail crossing with barrier](/road-signs/rail-crossing-with-barrier) shows a gate. [Rail crossing without barrier](/road-signs/rail-crossing-without-barrier) shows a steam train.

**Tell them apart:** a gate means a barrier will come down. A train means nothing will stop you, so spotting the train is your job. See [level crossing signs](/blogs/level-crossing-signs-kenya).

## 8. Turn right, and no right turn

The blue sign with a white arrow bending right, [vehicles turn right](/road-signs/vehicles-turn-right-junction), orders you to turn right. [No right turn](/road-signs/no-right-turn) has a black arrow bending right under a red bar, inside a red ring, and bans it.

**Tell them apart:** same arrow, opposite meaning. Blue says do it, red says don't. Read the colour before the picture.

## 9. Speed limit and minimum speed

The [speed limit](/road-signs/speed-limit) is a number in a red ring: the most you may do. [Compulsory minimum speed](/road-signs/compulsory-minimum-speed) is a number on blue: the least you may do.

**Tell them apart:** red is a ceiling, blue is a floor. The signs that end each one have diagonal lines through them. Our post on [speed signs](/blogs/speed-limit-signs-kenya) covers all four.

## 10. No parking and no stopping

[Parking prohibited](/road-signs/parking-prohibited) is a blue disc with a red ring and one red bar. [Parking or waiting prohibited](/road-signs/parking-or-waiting-prohibited) has a red X instead, and marks a clearway where you may not stop at all.

**Tell them apart:** count the red lines. One bar, don't park. An X, don't even stop. More in [parking and stopping signs](/blogs/parking-and-stopping-signs-kenya).

## The patterns behind the pairs

Look back over the list and three patterns explain almost all of it:

- **Mirror images.** Bends, slopes and narrowing roads come in left and right versions. Find the part of the picture that changes and ignore the rest.
- **Same picture, different colour.** Arrows, bicycles and numbers appear on blue signs and red ones with opposite meanings. Colour first, picture second.
- **One more line.** A second bar, a second turn in the arrow, a crossed-through number. The small extra mark is the whole difference.

Our class guides go through each group in detail: [warning signs](/road-signs/warning-signs), [mandatory signs](/road-signs/mandatory-signs) and [prohibitory signs](/road-signs/prohibitory-signs).

## How to actually fix this

Reading a list is not learning a sign. The signs that catch you are the ones you've **seen** but never **drilled**. The fix is naming each sign before you see the answer, and coming back to it over several days. We explain [why that works](/blogs/how-to-memorise-kenyan-road-signs). With look-alikes, drill both signs in a pair in the same session, so your brain has to tell them apart rather than recognise each one alone.

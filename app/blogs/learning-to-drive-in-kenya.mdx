Learning to drive in Kenya is not hard. It looks hard because the steps are spread across a website, a driving school and a test centre. This is the clean version, in the order it happens. DriveRush is not affiliated with the NTSA, so use the linked NTSA pages to confirm current rules and fees.

The part DriveRush helps with is the road signs. You use it alongside your driving school, never instead of one.

## The five steps, in order

1. **Enrol at a licensed driving school.** NTSA says the learner is required to enrol into a licensed driving school, and you must be 18 or over. The school must be registered and licensed by NTSA and must display its licence. See [choosing a driving school in Kenya](/driving-schools-kenya).
2. **Apply for the Provisional Driving Licence (PDL).** Through eCitizen, in the NTSA service portal. The fee is KES 650 and it is valid for 3 months. Put L plates on the front and rear, visible from at least 20 metres. Walkthrough: [how to apply for an NTSA driving licence in Kenya in 2026](/blogs/ntsa-driving-licence-kenya-2026).
3. **Learn the road signs and the Highway Code alongside your lessons.** Drill the signs until naming them is instant. Free trainers: <Link to="/road-signs/pelican">the Pelican chart</Link> and <Link to="/road-signs/3d">3D signs</Link>. For what the test covers, see the [NTSA test guide](/ntsa-test).
4. **Book the driver test.** After your PDL has matured for 21 days, book on eCitizen for KES 1,050, choose a date and go to your chosen test centre. Your school judges when you're ready and helps with booking. If you don't pass, you apply for a retest.
5. **Get your licence.** After you pass, apply for the Interim Driving Licence (KES 750, valid 6 months, printed by you). Then apply for the Smart DL (KES 3,050, 5 working days) and collect it at a Huduma centre or NTSA after the SMS. Renewals and replacements: [Smart DL guide](/blogs/smart-dl-replace-renew-lost).

## What it costs

Official NTSA fees for one licence total KES 5,500 (650 + 1,050 + 750 + 3,050). The driving school fee is on top and varies by school. AA Kenya's own site, for example, lists first-time B1/B2 Regular at KSh 24,900 and Premier at KSh 50,000. Read more: [what it costs to learn to drive](/blogs/cost-of-learning-to-drive-nairobi).

## Which licence class should you learn?

Most learners want a car licence, Class B. Choose the class before you enrol, because your school and your PDL application both depend on it.

- **Class A: motorbike.** Two- and three-wheelers, including boda bodas.
- **Class B: light vehicle.** Cars. This is the class most learners go for. Schools may split their car courses into sub-classes (AA Kenya offers B1, B2 and B3 for saloon cars), so ask your school which one applies to you.
- **Class C and D:** heavier goods and public service vehicles. These have more requirements and are not where most first-time learners start.

Full breakdown: [Class B, C or D? Which Kenyan driving licence you actually need](/blogs/class-b-c-d-licence-kenya).

## Built for Kenyan roads, not generic

Most online driving content is American or British. The signs are different, the test is different, the road is different. DriveRush is built for Kenya:

Kenya does not have one driving licence. It has classes, and the class you apply for decides what you may legally drive. This post keeps to what we can source. DriveRush is not affiliated with the NTSA, so confirm the exact class list and requirements with NTSA and your driving school.

## The classes in plain terms

**Motorcycles.** If you ride a boda boda, you need a motorcycle class. AA Kenya, for example, lists an A2 motorcycle course on its own site.

**Cars (Class B).** Saloon cars and similar light vehicles. This is what most first-time learners apply for. Schools often divide car courses into sub-classes: AA Kenya offers B1, B2 and B3 for saloon cars. We don't state here which sub-class is manual or automatic, because sources conflict. Ask your school which one matches the car you'll drive and learn in.

**Heavier goods vehicles, trailers and passenger service vehicles (matatus, buses).** These have their own classes, usually with extra requirements. They are not where most first-time learners start.

## How to pick

Three honest questions, in order.

**1. What will I drive most often this year?** If the answer is "a saloon I share with my family", that points to Class B. If it's a work van or pickup, ask your school which class covers it before you assume.

**2. Will my employer require a different class?** If your job involves driving a heavier vehicle, ask your employer which class they need before you enrol.

**3. Am I planning to do passenger-service work?** If yes, expect extra requirements on top of a basic licence. Ask NTSA or your school what they are. Start with the class you need now.

## The mistake most people make

Over-applying. Someone wants to be ready for "anything" and picks a heavier class "just in case", then trains on a vehicle they won't drive again. Apply for the class you'll actually drive.

## Then what?

Your class is chosen at the PDL stage, after you enrol at a licensed driving school. NTSA says the learner is required to enrol into one. See [choosing a driving school in Kenya](/driving-schools-kenya) and the [NTSA test guide](/ntsa-test).

Whatever class you're chasing, road signs and the Highway Code matter. If you haven't drilled signs yet, [the Pelican chart](/road-signs/pelican) is the fastest entry point (free, no signup), and [3D signs](/road-signs/3d) teach you to read them from any angle.

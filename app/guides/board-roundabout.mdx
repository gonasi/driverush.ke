The roundabout is where most mistakes on the Model Town Board are made, and nearly all of them come from being in the wrong lane. NTSA's learner handbook gives each lane a job. Learn the table below and the rest follows.

## Which lane for which turn

Lanes are numbered from the kerb inwards. Lane 1 is beside the kerb and lane 4 is beside the central reserve. On a four-lane road the handbook gives each lane a set number of options:

| Lane | Options | What it may do                                                                                                             |
| ---- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | 2       | Go straight (0°) or turn left (90°)                                                                                              |
| 2    | 1       | Go straight (0°)                                                                                                           |
| 3    | 2       | Go straight (0°) or turn right (90°)                                                                                       |
| 4    | 4       | Turn right (90°), come back (180°), turn left the long way round (270°), or go straight after going round (360°)           |

Read the other way, turn by turn:

- **To turn left,** use lane 1. It is the first exit.
- **To go straight,** use lane 1, 2 or 3. It is the second exit.
- **To turn right,** use lane 3 or 4. It is the third exit.
- **To come back the way you came,** use lane 4.

Lane 4 can reach every exit, because it is the only lane that may go all the way round. For a left turn or straight on it is the long way, so it is not the lane to choose for them.

## The option codes: 2124 and 215

Instructors teach the lane table as a code. Count the options in each lane, starting at the kerb:

- **2124** is the four-lane road: lane 1 has two options, lane 2 has one, lane 3 has two and lane 4 has four.
- **215** is the three-lane road: lane 1 has two options, lane 2 has one and lane 3 has five.

## The three-lane road: 215

| Lane | Options | What it may do                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | 2       | Go straight (0°) or turn left (90°)                                                                                                                                               |
| 2    | 1       | Go straight (0°)                                                                                                                                                            |
| 3    | 5       | Go straight (0°), turn right into lane 3, turn right into lane 4 (90°), come back into lane 3 (180°), or turn left into lane 3 and move to lane 4 on the broken line (270°) |

Lane 3 is the innermost lane of the three-lane road, so it does the work lane 4 does on the four-lane road.

## Choose your lane early

Close to the roundabout the lane lines are continuous, and a continuous white line means no changing lanes. Further back the lines are broken, and that is where you move across: check your mirrors, signal for 3 seconds, then move. If you reach the continuous lines in the wrong lane, you have to take a turn that lane allows and find the longest correct route from there.

## On the roundabout

You keep left in Kenya, so you go round clockwise. Give way to traffic already on the roundabout, which comes from your right. The lanes keep their numbers: lane 1 is the outer lane and lane 4 is the one beside the island.

The handbook lists five things you must not do on the roundabout:

- stop
- change lanes
- park
- overtake
- wait

## Going all the way round

Only the innermost lane may make a full circle: lane 4 from the four-lane road, lane 3 from the three-lane road. In the other lanes you take your exit the first time you reach it.

## Leaving the roundabout

Leave in the lane you came round in, and signal left as you go. Where the road you leave by has fewer lanes, take its innermost lane. Change lanes only further on, where the lines are broken.

## The three common mistakes

The handbook names them:

1. Approaching in the wrong lane.
2. Leaving in the wrong lane.
3. Changing lanes on the roundabout.

## What to ask your driving school

- **Lane 4 going straight (360°).** The handbook says lane 4 goes straight "after going around the roundabout". Ask whether your examiner wants one whole circle first. Our trainer accepts lane 4 leaving at the straight-on exit.
- **Which lane a full circle ends in.** Our trainer has you leave in the lane you came round in, which is what the handbook says for the three-lane road. Some instructors teach rejoining lane 1 after a full circle.
- **Their own board.** Boards are not all painted the same, so have your school walk you through 2124 and 215 on theirs.

## Practise it

The roundabout unit of the <Link to="/model-town-board">Model Town Board simulator</Link>
teaches the roundabout rules, shows each turn driven a step at a time, and then
gives you seven roundabout tasks to drive yourself, from the dual carriageway
and the three-lane road. It stops you when you take a turn your lane does not
allow and tells you which lane to use, and it takes a star when you leave in the
wrong lane. It is free, with no signup.

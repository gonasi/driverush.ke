The roundabout is where most mistakes on the Model Town Board are made, and nearly all of them come from being in the wrong lane. NTSA's learner handbook gives each lane a job. Learn the table below and the rest follows.

## Which lane for which turn

Lanes are numbered from the kerb inwards. Lane 1 is beside the kerb and lane 4 is beside the central reserve. On a four-lane road the handbook gives each lane its own job:

| Use lane  | To                            |
| --------- | ----------------------------- |
| 1         | Turn left (the first exit)    |
| 1, 2 or 3 | Go straight (the second exit) |
| 3 or 4    | Turn right (the third exit)   |
| 4         | Come back the way you came    |
| 4 only    | Go all the way round          |

Read the other way, lane by lane:

- **Lane 1** goes straight or turns left.
- **Lane 2** goes straight only.
- **Lane 3** goes straight or turns right.
- **Lane 4** turns right, comes back, or goes round.

Count the exits as you go round: left is the first, straight on is the second, right is the third.

## Choose your lane early

Close to the roundabout the lane lines are continuous, and a continuous white line means no changing lanes. Further back the lines are broken, and that is where you move across. If you reach the continuous lines in the wrong lane, you have to take a turn that lane allows.

## On the roundabout

You keep left in Kenya, so you go round clockwise. The lanes keep their numbers: lane 1 is the outer lane and lane 4 is the one beside the island.

The handbook lists five things you must not do on the roundabout:

- stop
- change lanes
- park
- overtake
- wait

## Going all the way round

Only lane 4, the innermost lane, may make a full circle. In lanes 1, 2 and 3 you take your exit the first time you reach it.

## The three common mistakes

The handbook names them:

1. Approaching in the wrong lane.
2. Leaving in the wrong lane.
3. Changing lanes on the roundabout.

## What to ask your driving school

- **Which lane to leave in.** The handbook counts leaving in the wrong lane as a mistake. Our trainer has you leave in the lane with the same number you came round in. Check that with your instructor.
- **The three-lane road.** The handbook's table for a three-lane approach is harder to read than the four-lane one, so we have not written it up here. Ask your school to walk you through it on their board.
- **Who gives way.** The handbook does not spell out right of way lane by lane.

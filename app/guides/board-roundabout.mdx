The roundabout is where most mistakes on the Model Town Board are made. NTSA's learner handbook calls it a meeting point where more than two roads meet, there to let vehicles go their different ways without blocking or hitting each other. These are its rules.

## Which lane to be in

Choose your lane before the continuous lines, because you may not change lanes once you reach them, or on the roundabout. Each lane has its own options, counted in the handbook's two codes: **2124** on the four-lane road and **215** on the three-lane road. In short, lane 1 turns left or goes straight, lane 2 goes straight only, and the innermost lane is the one for turning right and coming back.

<Link to="/model-town-board/lane-options">Model Town Board lane options</Link> has every option for both roads, with a picture of each car's line.

## On the roundabout

You keep left in Kenya, so you go round clockwise. Give way to traffic already on the roundabout, which comes from your right. The lanes keep their numbers: lane 1 is the outer lane and lane 4 is the one beside the island.

The handbook lists five things you must not do on the roundabout:

- stop
- change lanes
- park
- overtake
- wait

## The three parts of the roundabout

The handbook divides the roundabout into three parts:

- **The traffic island,** the green part in the middle, which controls how vehicles move round it.
- **The innermost lane,** lane 4. It is the only lane that may make a full circle (360°) from the four-lane road, and the only one that may go as far as 270° from the three-lane road.
- **The space,** lanes 1, 2 and 3. Cars in these lanes may not make a full circle: they take their exit the first time they reach it.

## Leaving the roundabout

Leave in the lane you came round in, and signal left as you go. Where the road you leave by has fewer lanes, take its innermost lane. Change lanes only further on, where the lines are broken.

## The common mistakes

The handbook names four:

1. Approaching in the wrong lane.
2. Leaving in the wrong lane.
3. Changing lanes on the roundabout.
4. Misreading the traffic lights, where a roundabout has them. Our board has none.

## What to ask your driving school

- **Who gives way.** We teach giving way to traffic already on the roundabout, which comes from your right. The handbook's Unit 3 does not say it, so ask your instructor how your test centre wants it done.
- **Lane 4 and full circles.** <Link to="/model-town-board/lane-options">The lane options page</Link> lists what to ask about lane 4 going straight and where a full circle ends.
- **Their own board.** Boards are not all painted the same, so have your school walk you round theirs.

## Practise it

The roundabout unit of the <Link to="/model-town-board">Model Town Board simulator</Link>
teaches the roundabout rules, shows each turn driven a step at a time, and then
gives you seven roundabout tasks to drive yourself, from the dual carriageway
and the three-lane road. It stops you when you take a turn your lane does not
allow and tells you which lane to use, and it takes a star when you leave in the
wrong lane. It is free, with no signup.

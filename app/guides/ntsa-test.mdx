To get a Kenyan driving licence, you enrol at a licensed driving school, get a Provisional Driving Licence, book and pass the NTSA test, then get an interim licence and a Smart DL. NTSA's own fees for those steps add up to **KES 5,500**. Your school's fee is separate.

DriveRush is not part of the NTSA and is not affiliated with it. The facts here come from the sources listed at the bottom of this page, and where we couldn't confirm something, we say so.

## The steps and what each one costs

| Step                           | What happens                                                                                     | NTSA fee     |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------ |
| 1. Enrol at a driving school   | NTSA requires a licensed school. You must be 18 or over.                                         | School's own |
| 2. Provisional Driving Licence | Valid for 3 months. Show L plates that can be seen from at least 20 metres, front and rear.      | KES 650      |
| 3. Book the test               | Once your PDL has matured for 21 days. Pay, then choose your date and test centre.               | KES 1,050    |
| 4. Sit the test                | At your chosen centre. If you don't pass, apply for a retest.                                    | Included     |
| 5. Interim Driving Licence     | After you pass. Print it yourself. Valid for 6 months.                                           | KES 750      |
| 6. Smart Driving Licence       | Takes about 5 working days. You get an SMS, then collect it from a Huduma Centre or NTSA office. | KES 3,050    |

You do all of this online: sign in to [eCitizen](https://ecitizen.go.ke), then open the NTSA service portal. If a guide tells you to use TIMS, it's out of date. NTSA moved its services to eCitizen in January 2024.

Pay only through the portal. In September 2026, NTSA warned about fraudsters posing as driving school agents and publishing inflated fees. If someone quotes you different NTSA fees, check the table above.

## What the NTSA test covers

According to [Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/114795-how-pass-ntsa-driving-test-kenya-model-town-board-highway-code-explained), the test covers two main areas, and your driving school judges when you're ready and helps you book:

- **The Model Town Board.** A scaled-down road network used to test lanes, roundabouts and parking. [Drive the Model Town Board](/model-town-board) in our free trainer, or read its [questions and answers](/model-town-board/questions).
- **The Highway Code.** The rules for all road users, including road signs and signals, speed limits, overtaking, right of way, and using signals and mirrors.

Road signs are the part you can start on today. We have [83 Kenyan road signs and their meanings](/road-signs), and two free trainers for memorising them: the <Link to="/road-signs/pelican">Pelican chart</Link> and <Link to="/road-signs/3d">3D signs</Link>.

## What we couldn't confirm

NTSA's service pages don't describe the format of the test, and other sources disagree. Some practice sites describe a computer-based paper of 20 questions with a pass mark of 16. Another guide describes an oral or written test with a pass mark of about 70%.

We can't confirm any of those numbers, so we won't tell you to rely on them. Ask your driving school how the test runs at your centre.

Mandatory signs are blue circles with a white symbol, and they tell you what you must do. There are 10 on the chart. Where a warning sign says "be ready" and a prohibitory sign says "don't", a mandatory sign says "do this". If you see a blue disc, it's an order, not a suggestion.

They are the smallest of the main classes, which makes them a quick win. They're also the class most often confused with the prohibitory signs, because the same arrows and pictures appear on both, with opposite meanings.

## Blue means you must

The Ministry of Roads road signs manual splits regulatory signs into two kinds. Mandatory signs give instructions that must be obeyed, and most are circular with a white symbol and border on a blue background. Prohibitory signs tell you what you must not do, and most are circular with a red border.

That gives you a simple rule for any round sign:

- **Blue background, white picture:** do what the picture shows.
- **White background, red ring:** don't do what the picture shows, or keep to the limit on it.

The manual also counts [Stop](/road-signs/stop) and [Give way](/road-signs/give-way) as mandatory, since they're instructions you must follow. On the chart and in the trainers they have their own class, priority signs, because their shapes are unique.

## The three groups

**Which way to go.** [Vehicles travel straight](/road-signs/vehicles-travel-straight), [vehicles turn right](/road-signs/vehicles-turn-right-junction), the direction to be followed, the straight-or-right sign and [pass to the left](/road-signs/pass-to-the-left). Each shows a white arrow, and the arrow is the whole message: the number of arrowheads tells you how many choices you have.

**Who the lane is for.** [Compulsory cycle track](/road-signs/compulsory-cycle-track), the compulsory footpath for pedestrians, and the compulsory track for cattle. The picture tells you who the path belongs to. Everyone else stays off it.

**How slow you may go.** [Compulsory minimum speed](/road-signs/compulsory-minimum-speed) and its [end sign](/road-signs/end-of-compulsory-minimum-speed). These are the only mandatory signs with a number on them.

## Arrows: count the heads, read the angle

The arrow signs are where most points are lost.

- **One straight arrow up** means straight on is your only choice. Both turns are ruled out.
- **One arrow that bends** means you must turn that way. A bend in the arrow means a turn at a junction.
- **An arrow that splits** gives you a choice between the directions it shows, and nothing else.
- **An arrow slanting down to the left** isn't about turning at all. It tells you which side to pass something, like a traffic island in front of you. Keep left of it.

Before you signal, count the arrowheads. One head means one way out.

## Blue against red: same picture, opposite meaning

The easiest way to lose a mark is to read the picture and skip the colour.

- **A white arrow turning right on blue** orders you to turn right. A black right-turn arrow under a red bar, inside a red ring, is [no right turn](/road-signs/no-right-turn). Same arrow, opposite meaning.
- **A bicycle on blue** sets a track aside for bicycles. A bicycle in a red ring with a slash bans them.
- **A number on blue** is a minimum speed: you must go at least that fast. A number in a red ring is a [speed limit](/road-signs/speed-limit): you may not go faster. Treat the blue number as a floor and the red one as a ceiling.

## Minimum speed, and where it ends

The minimum speed sign keeps very slow vehicles off roads where they would hold up fast traffic and become a danger. It never means you should drive faster than is safe: if traffic, weather or visibility make the number unsafe, conditions come first.

The manual shows the sign that ends a minimum speed: the same blue disc, crossed through with red. After it you may go slower again, but any maximum speed limit still applies. Don't confuse it with the [end of speed limit](/road-signs/end-of-speed-limit) sign, which is white with black lines and ends a maximum.

## Tracks for cyclists, people and cattle

The manual describes the cycle track sign as marking the start of a track reserved for pedal cycles, used together with cycle-track road markings, and says motor vehicles are not allowed on it. The footpath and cattle track signs follow the same idea: the path is for whoever is drawn on the sign.

For a driver, the lesson is to keep off, and to watch where these paths cross your road at junctions and driveways, because whoever uses them may come across your path.

## How to learn them

With only 10 signs, mandatory signs make a good first round on the [Pelican chart trainer](/road-signs/pelican). Then drill them mixed in with the [prohibitory signs](/road-signs/prohibitory-signs), since telling blue from red with the same picture is what the test is really checking. The [3D trainer](/road-signs/3d) helps you read the arrows from an angle, as you would at a real junction.

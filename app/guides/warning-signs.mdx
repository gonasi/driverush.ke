Warning signs are the biggest class on the chart: 34 of the {SIGN_COUNT} signs. They are triangles with a red border, point up, with a black picture on white. A warning sign never orders you to do anything. It tells you what is coming, so you have time to slow down and get ready before you reach it.

That makes them easy to recognise and hard to master. The shape gives the class away from far off, but the pictures are small, many come in mirror-image pairs, and the test expects you to name the exact one.

## Read the shape first, then the picture

Every Kenyan road sign gives its job away by shape and colour before you can make out the symbol:

- **A red triangle, point up,** warns. Something is ahead.
- **A blue circle** orders. See [mandatory signs](/road-signs/mandatory-signs).
- **A red ring** forbids or sets a limit. See [prohibitory signs](/road-signs/prohibitory-signs).
- **A triangle standing on its point** is [Give way](/road-signs/give-way), and the octagon is [Stop](/road-signs/stop). Those two are priority signs, not warnings, even though Give way is a triangle too.

So when you see a triangle, you already know two things: nobody is giving you an order, and you should ease off and look for the thing in the picture.

## The five groups

Learning 34 signs one by one is slow. Grouping them by what they warn about makes each new sign easier to place.

**The shape of the road.** [Bend left](/road-signs/bend-left), [bend right](/road-signs/bend-right), [successive bends to the left](/road-signs/successive-bends-to-the-left) and [to the right](/road-signs/successive-bends-to-the-right), [steep descent](/road-signs/steep-descent-ahead) and [steep ascent](/road-signs/steep-ascent-ahead), and the three narrowing signs: [both sides](/road-signs/road-narrows-ahead), [from the right](/road-signs/road-narrows-from-right) and [from the left](/road-signs/road-narrows-from-left). These show the road as a thick black line or a pair of strips, so read the line like a map of what's ahead.

**The surface.** [Uneven road](/road-signs/uneven-road-surface-ahead), [severe bump](/road-signs/severe-bump-ahead), [severe dip](/road-signs/severe-dip-ahead), [slippery road](/road-signs/slippery-road) and loose surface. Each one is about how the road will feel under your tyres, so each one means slowing before you reach it, not on it.

**People and animals.** [Pedestrian crossing](/road-signs/pedestrian-crossing), [children crossing](/road-signs/children-crossing-ahead), cyclists, [cattle](/road-signs/cattle-crossing-ahead) and [wild animals](/road-signs/wild-animals). These are about something that moves, so the danger can be anywhere near the sign, not at one exact point.

**Junctions and traffic.** [Road junction](/road-signs/road-junction-ahead), the junction signs that show a road joining or a stop ahead, [traffic lights](/road-signs/traffic-light-signals-ahead), [two-way traffic](/road-signs/two-way-traffic-ahead) and [roundabout](/road-signs/roundabout-ahead). These warn you that someone else will soon share your space.

**Crossings and one-offs.** [Rail crossing with barrier](/road-signs/rail-crossing-with-barrier), [rail crossing without barrier](/road-signs/rail-crossing-without-barrier), [river bank or quayside](/road-signs/river-bank-or-quayside), [falling rocks](/road-signs/danger-of-falling-and-fallen-rocks-ahead), [road works](/road-signs/road-works-ahead), [low-flying aircraft](/road-signs/low-flying-aircraft-crossing-ahead) and the general hazard sign, an exclamation mark that names no particular danger.

## The pairs people mix up

Most mistakes with warning signs aren't about not knowing a sign. They're about reading the right sign backwards.

- **Bend left and bend right.** Look at where the top of the black line leans. It points the way the road goes.
- **One bend or several.** A single kink is one bend. A zigzag is a series, and the first step of the zigzag shows the first bend.
- **Steep descent and steep ascent.** Follow the slope from left to right and look at the little car's nose: tipped down is a descent, tipped up is a climb.
- **Which side narrows.** On the one-sided narrowing signs, find the strip that stays straight. The other side is the one closing in.
- **Bump, dip and uneven road.** One hump rising out of the road is a bump. One hollow cut into it is a dip. Two humps mean a rough stretch that goes on.
- **Pedestrians and children.** One adult walking over stripes is a crossing. Two children holding hands, with no stripes, means children may cross anywhere nearby.
- **Cattle and wild animals.** A standing cow usually comes with a herder. A leaping antelope means wildlife with nobody controlling it.
- **The two rail crossings.** A gate means a barrier should come down. A steam train means there's no barrier, and spotting the train is your job.

## How far ahead the warning comes

Warning signs are put up before the hazard, not at it. The Ministry of Roads road signs manual says bigger warning signs are used on faster roads and placed further back from the hazard, so that a driver has time to take in the message and finish slowing down or moving over before reaching it.

That's why the right response is to act on the straight, as soon as you see the sign. On a bend, slow down before you turn in, not halfway round. Before a dip or a bump, brake while the road is still flat.

The same manual describes how junction warning signs show which road matters more: the arms of the road symbol are drawn at different thicknesses, and the thicker arm is the more important road. If the road you're on is drawn thick and the side road thin, you are on the main road, but you should still expect traffic to come out.

## How to learn them

Grouped like this, the 34 warning signs become five small sets. Learn one group at a time on the [Pelican chart trainer](/road-signs/pelican), say each name out loud before you reveal it, and come back on a different day to see which ones stuck. Spin the same signs in [3D](/road-signs/3d) to practise naming them from an angle, the way you'll actually see them from the driver's seat. For the method behind this, read [how to memorise Kenyan road signs](/blogs/how-to-memorise-kenyan-road-signs).

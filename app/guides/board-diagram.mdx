## How the lanes are numbered

Lanes are counted from the kerb. Lane 1 is beside the kerb, and the highest number is beside the middle of the road. The dual carriageway has four lanes each way, so lane 4 runs beside the central reserve. The three-lane road has three each way, so lane 3 runs beside its yellow centre line. The roundabout's lanes are counted the same way, from the outside in.

The arrows painted in each lane show where that lane may go. The handbook sums them up as an option code: **2124** on the four-lane road and **215** on the three-lane road. <Link to="/model-town-board/lane-options">The lane options page</Link> explains both.

## How to read the board

Everything painted on the board is an instruction. Read it the way you would read a real road:

- **The lines between lanes.** A broken white line means you may change lanes if the road is clear. A continuous white line means you may not. Near the roundabout the lines turn continuous, so your lane is chosen before you reach them.
- **The line down the middle.** On the two-way road, a continuous yellow line means stay on your side. A broken yellow line means you may overtake or turn across it when the road is clear.
- **The yellow kerb.** It is the edge of the road. No stopping, parking or waiting beside it.
- **The central reserve.** It keeps the two directions of the dual carriageway apart. A gap in it is where a car can turn back.
- **Stop lines, give way lines and crossings.** Stop at a stop line even when nothing is coming. Give way to the major road at a give way line. Never stop on a pedestrian crossing.
- **The parking zone.** Angle bays and flush boxes. You drive through it only when there is no other correct route.

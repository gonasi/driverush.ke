The Model Town Board is a scaled-down road network that Kenyan driving schools use to teach road rules and traffic flow. It's also one of the two main areas of the NTSA test, alongside the Highway Code, according to a [Kenyans.co.ke guide to the test](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/114795-how-pass-ntsa-driving-test-kenya-model-town-board-highway-code-explained).

We're building an interactive Model Town Board trainer, but it isn't ready yet. Until it is, this page explains how the board works, and you can get the signs on it solid today.

## What's on the board

The board packs the situations you'll meet on a real road into one small town. It helps you understand:

- roundabouts
- one-way and two-way traffic
- pedestrian crossings
- parking

Boards aren't all identical, so ask your driving school to show you the one you'll be tested on and to practise on it with you.

## The three route rules

Kenyans.co.ke reports three rules from NTSA for finding your way around the board. Learn them in this order:

1. **Take the shortest correct route,** avoiding the parking area.
2. **If that isn't possible, take the longest correct route,** still avoiding the parking area.
3. **Use the parking area only as a last resort.**

"Correct" is the important word. A short route that breaks a rule, such as going the wrong way down a one-way road, isn't a correct route at all.

## What you're tested on

The same guide lists three things candidates are tested on:

- **Lane discipline.** Choosing the correct lane for each turn before you make it.
- **Roundabout rules.** Knowing when to give way and how to exit correctly.
- **Parking.** Showing methods such as parallel parking.

Treat the small car the way you'd treat a real one. Obey every sign on the route in order, and don't take shortcuts you wouldn't take on a real road.

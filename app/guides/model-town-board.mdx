The Model Town Board is a scaled-down road network that Kenyan driving schools use to teach road rules and traffic flow. It's also one of the two main areas of the NTSA test, alongside the Highway Code, according to a [Kenyans.co.ke guide to the test](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/114795-how-pass-ntsa-driving-test-kenya-model-town-board-highway-code-explained).

You can practise the board in the trainer at the top of this page. Learn it first, by watching a car drive any lane, route or parking move a step at a time. Then drive it yourself: the roundabout, the street, the two-way road and the parking bays. It tells you which rule you broke when it stops you. The rest of this page explains how the board works and the signs you need on it.

## Learn the board first

<Link to="/model-town-board?learn=1">Open the board to learn it</Link>, with no task and no clock. There are four things to look at:

- **Lanes.** Pick a road into the roundabout and a lane, and see every turn that lane may make. Tap a turn and a car drives it.
- **Board.** Tap anything painted on the board, such as a stop line or the flush parking, to see it and what it asks of you.
- **Routes.** Watch routes across the whole board, from easy to hard, with the rule for each step.
- **Parking.** Watch a car park in an angle bay and reverse out of it, and reverse into flush parking and pull out.

You can slide the view with a finger and pinch to zoom, and play any route slowly or a step at a time.

## How the trainer works

The board is on a table, as it is at a driving school, and you stand at one side of it. A yellow car waits on the road nearest you.

- **The examiner gives you a task,** such as "Turn right at the roundabout. Stop in the green box."
- **You drive with one joystick.** Push it toward where you want the car to go and let go to stop. Arrow keys work on a computer.
- **A small mistake costs a star** and you carry on: bumping a kerb, or changing lanes over a continuous line.
- **A serious mistake stops you,** and you are told the rule: the wrong lane for your turn, the wrong exit, or driving against the traffic.
- **You can stand at any side of the table.** The gear button moves you to the top, bottom, left or right, and switches lane assist on or off.
- **You can ask for a hint.** "Next step" lights the road ahead and gives its rule. "Show me" drives the whole task for you to watch. A task passed with a hint comes round again.
- **Signs come up as you reach them.** Near a give way line, a stop line or a pedestrian crossing, the sign and what it means show until you have gone by.
- **The parking tasks have a reverse gear.** Stop, pull the joystick back and hold it, and the back of the car goes where you point.

There are 13 tasks so far: six at the roundabout and on the dual carriageway, three on the street and the two-way road, and four for parking. The trainer is not the test: at a test centre you move a toy car by hand on a real board. It is a way to get the lane rules into your head first.

## What's on the board

The board packs the situations you'll meet on a real road into one small town. It helps you understand:

- roundabouts
- one-way and two-way traffic
- pedestrian crossings
- parking

NTSA's learner handbook lists the features of the board: one-way and two-way roads, a roundabout, parking zones, the yellow kerb, a pedestrian crossing, a stop sign, a give way sign and road markings such as arrows.

## Which lane for which turn

Lanes are numbered from the outside in. Lane 1 is beside the kerb, and the highest number is beside the centre of the road. The handbook gives each lane on a four-lane road its own job at the roundabout:

| Lane | What it may do at the roundabout |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Go straight or turn left. |
| 2 | Go straight only. |
| 3 | Go straight or turn right. |
| 4 | Turn right, come back the way you came, or go round. It is the only lane that may make a full circle. |

Choose your lane before you reach the roundabout, because you can't change once you're on it. <Link to="/model-town-board/roundabout">Which lane for which turn</Link> goes through it turn by turn.

## The roundabout rules

You keep left and go round clockwise. The handbook lists five things you must not do on the roundabout:

- stop
- change lanes
- park
- overtake
- wait

It also names the common mistakes: approaching in the wrong lane, leaving in the wrong lane, and changing lanes on the roundabout.

## The three route rules

NTSA's learner handbook gives three rules for finding your way around the board, and Kenyans.co.ke reports the same three. Learn them in this order:

1. **Take the shortest correct route,** avoiding the parking area.
2. **If that isn't possible, take the longest correct route,** still avoiding the parking area.
3. **Use the parking area only as a last resort.**

"Correct" is the important word. A short route that breaks a rule, such as going the wrong way down a one-way road, isn't a correct route at all. <Link to="/model-town-board/rules">The Model Town Board rules</Link> has the rest, and <Link to="/model-town-board/parking">parking on the board</Link> has angle and flush parking.

## What you're tested on

The same guide lists three things candidates are tested on:

- **Lane discipline.** Choosing the correct lane for each turn before you make it.
- **Roundabout rules.** Knowing when to give way and how to exit correctly.
- **Parking.** Showing methods such as parallel parking.

Treat the small car the way you'd treat a real one. Obey every sign on the route in order, and don't take shortcuts you wouldn't take on a real road.

## The signs you need on the board

You can't route around a town if you can't read its signs. These are the ones to get solid first. Each link explains the sign and lets you practise it:

- <Link to="/road-signs/stop">Stop</Link>: you must stop, even with no line on
  the road and no traffic in sight.
- <Link to="/road-signs/give-way">Give way</Link>: traffic on the major road
  goes first.
- <Link to="/road-signs/no-entry">No entry</Link>: you can't go in from this
  side, so it rules out a route.
- <Link to="/road-signs/roundabout-ahead">Roundabout ahead</Link>: get ready to
  give way.
- <Link to="/road-signs/pedestrian-crossing">Pedestrian crossing</Link>: slow
  down and be ready to stop.
- <Link to="/road-signs/two-way-traffic-ahead">Two-way traffic ahead</Link>:
  traffic will be coming toward you.
- <Link to="/road-signs/parking-prohibited">Parking prohibited</Link>: you may

## Practice questions

Test yourself on the rules above. The answers come from the route rules and test areas described on this page.

    The shorter one, as long as it avoids the parking area.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem summary="The only short route cuts through the parking area, but a longer route avoids it. Which do you take?">
    The longer route. You should only use the parking area as a last resort.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem summary="When can you drive through the parking area?">
    Only when no correct route avoids it.
  </AccordionItem>

    No. It breaks a rule, so it isn't a correct route, however short it is.

  <AccordionItem summary="There's a STOP sign on your route, but no traffic and no painted line. What do you do?">

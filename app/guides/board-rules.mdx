The Model Town Board is a small town drawn on a table, with a toy car you move along its roads. The rules are the rules of the road, shrunk to fit. These are the ones NTSA's learner handbook gives for the board.

## The three route rules

The examiner names a place to go. There is usually more than one way to get there, and the handbook gives three rules for choosing:

1. **Take the shortest correct route,** avoiding the parking area.
2. **If a continuous line blocks the short way from your lane, take the longest correct route,** still avoiding the parking area.
3. **Use the parking area only as a last resort.**

"Correct" is the word that matters. A short route that breaks a rule, such as crossing a continuous line or going the wrong way down a one-way road, is not a correct route at all.

Rule 2 is the one learners miss. Say you are in lane 2, the lines beside you are already continuous, and you are asked to turn left. Lane 2 only goes straight and you may not change lanes, so the short way is blocked. You go straight, turn back, and come round to the road you were asked for. That is the longest correct route.

## Keep left, and know your lane number

You drive on the left in Kenya. Lanes are numbered from the kerb inwards, so lane 1 is beside the kerb and the highest number is beside the middle of the road.

## Continuous lines and broken lines

- **A broken white line** between lanes means you may change lanes or overtake if the road is clear. Check your mirrors and signal for 3 seconds before you move.
- **A continuous white line** means you may not. On the board the lines turn continuous close to the roundabout, so choose your lane before you reach them.
- **A continuous yellow line** down the middle of a two-way road means stay on your side of it. No overtaking.
- **A broken yellow line** down the middle means you may overtake, or turn across it, if the road is clear.

## The roundabout

Give way to traffic already on the roundabout, which comes from your right. Go round clockwise, in the lane you chose, and leave in that lane. The handbook lists five things you must not do on it: stop, change lanes, park, overtake or wait. Only the innermost lane may go all the way round. The lane options are taught as two codes: 2124 for the four-lane road and 215 for the three-lane road. <Link to="/model-town-board/roundabout">Which lane for which turn</Link> has the full table.

## Kerbs and the central reserve

The handbook lists the yellow kerb among the board's features. Treat it as the edge of the road and keep the car off it. A yellow kerb means no stopping, parking or waiting beside it. The central reserve keeps the two directions of a dual carriageway apart. A gap in it is where a car can turn back or leave the main road. Be in the lane beside the reserve first, and give way to traffic on the side you are joining.

## Stop, give way and crossings

- At a <Link to="/road-signs/stop">Stop</Link> sign you stop, even with no line on the road and nothing coming. Look right, left, then right again, and go when the road is clear.
- At a <Link to="/road-signs/give-way">Give way</Link> sign, slow down, or stop if you need to. Traffic on the major road goes first.
- At a <Link to="/road-signs/pedestrian-crossing">pedestrian crossing</Link>, slow down and be ready to stop before it. Never stop, park or wait on the crossing itself.

Obey every sign on your route, in order. You can learn all {SIGN_COUNT} of them on the <Link to="/road-signs/pelican">Pelican chart</Link> or with <Link to="/road-signs/3d">3D signs</Link>.

## Parking

The board has angle parking and flush parking, and the route rules tell you to keep out of the parking area unless there is no other way. Angle parking is for small cars: take the farthest free bay, drive in forwards and reverse out. Flush parking takes any vehicle except a trailer or a tractor: take the farthest free box, reverse in and drive out forwards. <Link to="/model-town-board/parking">Parking on the Model Town Board</Link> covers both.

## What to ask your driving school

Boards are not all the same, and the handbook does not cover everything. Ask your instructor:

- to show you the board you will be tested on, and practise on it with you
- whether lane 4 must go once right round the roundabout before it leaves straight on
- which lane the middle lane of the three-lane road may turn from at a junction
- whether your test centre still follows the 2016 handbook

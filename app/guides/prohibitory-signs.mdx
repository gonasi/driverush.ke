Prohibitory signs tell you what you must not do, or set a limit you must keep. There are 33 on the chart, almost as many as the warning signs. Most are white circles with a red ring, and many have a red bar across the picture to show that the thing in it is banned.

They cover a lot of ground: which vehicles may enter, which turns you may make, how fast, how heavy, how tall, and where you may stop. That's why they reward learning in groups.

## Red ring means don't

The Ministry of Roads road signs manual describes prohibitory signs as instructions about moves that are not allowed, such as banned turns or entry, and lists speed limits, clearways and weight limits as further examples. It says most are circular, with a red border and a cancellation bar.

Set them against the other round signs:

- **White inside a red ring:** don't, or don't go over this limit.
- **Blue with white:** do this. See [mandatory signs](/road-signs/mandatory-signs).
- **Blue with a red ring and red bars:** the parking and stopping signs. These are prohibitory too, even though they're blue inside.

## The six groups

**Entry.** [No entry](/road-signs/no-entry) is a solid red disc with a white bar, and it closes a road from your side only, usually the wrong end of a one-way street. [Road closed](/road-signs/road-closed) is an empty red ring, and it bans every vehicle from both directions. Then there's a long run of bans on one kind of road user: motorcycles, bicycles, pedestrians, animal-drawn carts, hand carts, farm vehicles, heavy goods vehicles and power-driven vehicles. In each one, the picture in the ring is the thing that may not go in.

**Size and weight.** Limits on [height](/road-signs/no-entry-for-vehicles-exceeding-height-shown), width, length, [total weight](/road-signs/no-entry-for-vehicles-exceeding-weight-shown) and [weight on one axle](/road-signs/no-entry-for-vehicles-exceeding-weight-shown-on-one-axle). The figure tells you the limit, and the triangles or drawings around it tell you what's being measured.

**Turns.** [No left turn](/road-signs/no-left-turn), [no right turn](/road-signs/no-right-turn) and [no U-turn](/road-signs/no-u-turn). Read the full shape of the arrow, not just the bar.

**Overtaking and speed.** [No overtaking of four-wheeled vehicles](/road-signs/overtaking-of-four-wheeled-vehicles-prohibited), no overtaking of goods vehicles, the [speed limit](/road-signs/speed-limit) and the [end of speed limit](/road-signs/end-of-speed-limit), plus the sign that ends all earlier bans at once.

**Stopping and control.** The [school children stop sign](/road-signs/stop-children-school), the control post stop sign, priority for oncoming traffic, and the ban on horns.

**Parking.** [Parking prohibited](/road-signs/parking-prohibited) and [no stopping](/road-signs/parking-or-waiting-prohibited), plus the zone version.

## Numbers: read what comes after them

Several prohibitory signs carry a number, and the number means something different on each.

- **A number alone in a red ring** is a speed limit, in kilometres per hour.
- **A number followed by T** is a weight in tonnes, and it has nothing to do with speed. If there's an axle drawn under it, the limit is per axle, not for the whole vehicle.
- **A number between two triangles pointing in from above and below** is a height. Pointing in from the sides, it's a width.
- **The same number crossed through with thin black lines, on white with no red ring,** means that limit has ended.

## One bar or two

The two parking signs are blue with a red ring, and the difference is in the bars. The manual's no-parking sign has a single bar. Its no-stopping sign has a red X, and it marks a clearway, where you may not stop or park during the hours shown on a plate below. The end of a clearway is marked with the same sign and an END plate.

So count the red lines: one means don't park, two means don't even stop, not to drop someone off and not to answer the phone.

## Empty ring, solid disc

Two signs carry almost no picture at all, and they're easy to mix up.

- **[No entry](/road-signs/no-entry):** solid red, with a white bar across. You may not go in from this side, usually because traffic is coming towards you.
- **[Road closed](/road-signs/road-closed):** white inside, with just the red ring. The manual shows it with a pedestrian zone plate and opening hours below. No vehicle may enter from either end.

## Same picture, opposite meaning

Most of the pictures on prohibitory signs also turn up somewhere else. A bicycle in a red ring bans bicycles, but a bicycle on blue gives them a track. A number in a red ring is the most you may do, but a number on blue is the least. A right-turn arrow under a red bar bans the turn, but the same arrow in white on blue orders it. Always read the colour before the picture.

## How to learn them

Thirty-three is a lot in one sitting. Drill one group at a time on the [Pelican chart trainer](/road-signs/pelican), starting with the turns and the numbers, which come up on every drive. Then mix them with the [mandatory signs](/road-signs/mandatory-signs) to practise telling red from blue, and use the [3D trainer](/road-signs/3d) to read the figures from an angle. For a full study plan, see [how to memorise Kenyan road signs](/blogs/how-to-memorise-kenyan-road-signs).

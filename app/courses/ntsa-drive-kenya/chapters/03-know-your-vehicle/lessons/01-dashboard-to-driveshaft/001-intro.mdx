Sit in the driver's seat of any saloon in Kenya, a Toyota Vitz, a Mark X, a Probox, and you will find the same controls in roughly the same places. The shape changes. The job does not.

<MediaHint
  type="diagram"
  alt="Driver's cockpit of a saloon: steering wheel, instrument cluster, gear lever, handbrake, three pedals"
  caption="Your office for the next 40 years of driving."
  placement="inline"
/>

In front of you sits the **instrument cluster**: the speedometer, the fuel gauge, the temperature gauge, and a small army of warning lights. To your right or left depending on whether the car is Japan-import or not, sits the gear lever and the handbrake. Below your feet, three pedals do the heavy lifting.

<Callout type="analogy">
  Think of the cockpit like a doctor's consulting room. The dashboard is the
  patient's vitals. The pedals are your hands on the patient. Read first, act
  second.
</Callout>

This lesson walks the controls one by one. By the end you will know what each pedal does, what each warning light is shouting at you, and which gear belongs to which speed.

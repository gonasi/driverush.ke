In Kenya the law does not ask whether your car looks new. It asks whether your car is **roadworthy**. The Traffic Act puts the responsibility on the registered owner and the driver, jointly, to keep every system, brakes, lights, tyres, steering, exhaust, in working order. Drive a car you know is faulty and you carry the liability when it crashes.

<Rule>
  As driver or owner, you are legally responsible for the roadworthy condition
  of the vehicle every time it moves. Knowing about a fault and driving anyway
  is an offence in its own right.
</Rule>

Preventive maintenance, PM, is the cheap version of maintenance: small checks, small fixes, on a schedule. Reactive maintenance is the expensive version: waiting until something breaks on Mombasa Road, then paying for a tow, a new part, and the time you lost. Every shilling you spend on PM saves you ten in repairs.

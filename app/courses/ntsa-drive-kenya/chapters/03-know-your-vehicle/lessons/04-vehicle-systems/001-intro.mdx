Modern cars are smarter than they look. Behind every warning light sits a system, often a computer, and a sensor that has decided something is worth telling you. Two systems matter more than the rest, because they are the ones that save your life when things go wrong.

The first is **ABS**, the Anti-lock Braking System. ABS senses when a wheel is about to lock under hard braking and rapidly releases and re-applies the brake on that wheel, many times per second, so you can still steer while braking hard.

The second is **SRS**, the Supplementary Restraint System. That is the official name for airbags. SRS is built to work with the seatbelt, not instead of it. A driver hit by an airbag without a belt on can still die from the impact alone.

<Callout type="warning">
  An ABS or SRS warning light that stays on means the safety system is offline.
  The car will still drive, but the system that was meant to save you in a crash
  is not there. Get it checked the same day.
</Callout>

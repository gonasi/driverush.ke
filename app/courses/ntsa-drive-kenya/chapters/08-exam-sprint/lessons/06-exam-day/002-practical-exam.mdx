The practical exam is held on the NTSA model town board: a scale physical layout of a road network with roundabouts, parking bays, junctions, and a pedestrian crossing.

<Rule>
  The practical examiner judges three things: vehicle control, observation
  (mirrors and shoulder checks), and judgment (right of way, sign obedience,
  lane discipline).
</Rule>

You arrive, get assigned a vehicle, and the examiner reads out a route. You drive it, the examiner watches and ticks boxes. Typical elements include moving off and stopping, a roundabout, both parking types (angle and flush), a Stop sign procedure, and reading at least one sign aloud.

Common reasons candidates fail: skipping mirror checks, stalling on the move-off, parking outside the bay lines, missing a Stop sign procedure, or hitting a kerb.

<Callout type="tip">
  Talk through your actions out loud during the practical. Saying "checking
  mirror, signalling, moving off" tells the examiner you are observing even when
  they cannot see your eyes. Quiet candidates get marked down for missed
  observations they actually did.
</Callout>

Pass both and you get a provisional driver's licence. Then a probationary period before the full one.

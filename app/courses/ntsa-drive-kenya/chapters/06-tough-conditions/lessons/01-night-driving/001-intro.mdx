It's 9pm on the Nairobi-Nakuru highway. Your low beams light up roughly 30 metres of tarmac ahead of you. At 100 km/h, you cover that 30 metres in just over one second.

If a cow walks onto the road at the edge of your beam, you have one second to see, decide, and brake. You will not stop in time.

<Rule>
  Never outrun your headlights. Your safe speed at night is the speed at which
  you can stop within the distance your beams actually illuminate.
</Rule>

This single rule kills more night-time driving misconceptions than any other. Your headlights are not an excuse to drive at the same speed as the daytime limit. They are a visibility budget, and you must spend within it.

<Callout type="analogy">
  Daytime, you can see a kilometre ahead. Night, you see 30 to 100 metres. Same
  road, same car, but the stopping problem is a hundred times harder.
</Callout>

Difficult terrain rewards patience and punishes panic. Three short habits cover almost every situation you will meet on a rural Kenyan road.

Read the surface before you commit. If the patch ahead looks darker than the rest, it is probably wetter and softer. Pick a line through the shallowest part, ideally where vehicles ahead have already firmed up tracks.

When in doubt, lower gear, lower speed, lighter foot. Second gear at 20 km/h gets through almost anything a saloon car can survive. Higher gear at higher speed wins nothing and risks the differential when a tyre suddenly drops into a hole.

Help yourself out of trouble before you ask others. A piece of cardboard, a few stones, a branch, or even the floor mats will get most stuck saloons moving again. Carry a small shovel and a tow rope on long rural trips. They cost very little and save the day.

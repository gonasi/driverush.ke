Tarmac is the easy game. Most Kenyan kilometres are not tarmac. The C-class and D-class roads that link shopping centres to farms are gravel, black cotton soil, murram, or a wet-season mix of all three.

These surfaces ask different things of you. Grip is half what it is on tarmac, and sometimes a quarter. The road wants to throw your steering off-line, especially in deep ruts. A 4x4 only helps with traction: it does nothing for the laws of physics on a corner.

<Callout type="analogy">
  Tarmac driving is chess on a clean board. Rural driving is chess on a board
  that keeps tilting. The pieces are the same, but every move costs more.
</Callout>

Two specific hazards dominate the rural Kenyan map: deep mud that traps you, and rolling dust that hides everything ahead. We cover both in this lesson, plus the unwritten rule that holds it all together.

<Rule>
  On loose surfaces, every input is slower and softer than on tarmac. Steer
  earlier, brake earlier, accelerate gentler. Sudden anything ends in a slide.
</Rule>

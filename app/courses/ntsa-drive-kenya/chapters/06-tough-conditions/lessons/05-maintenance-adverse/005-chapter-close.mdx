You have closed out Chapter 6. Tough conditions are not a special talent reserved for taxi veterans and rally drivers. They are the everyday Kenyan drive: night, rain, dust, mud, and an older or newer brake system that you must know how to use.

<Mnemonic>
  Adapt the light. Adapt the speed. Adapt the gap. Adapt the foot. Adapt the car
  before you turn the key.
</Mnemonic>

Five short habits running through every lesson in this chapter:

Match your speed to what you can actually see, never to what the sign says.

Double your following distance the moment the road surface, light, or weather changes.

Know which braking technique your car needs, and practise it before you ever have to use it.

Check the engine bay before any tough-conditions trip, especially coolant, brake fluid, and belts.

Remember that the driver and owner share the legal duty for the machine. Walking around the car for 60 seconds before a long, hard drive is not optional.

Next chapter steps up the stakes again: cargo, hazards, and what to do when an emergency happens despite all your preparation. See you there.

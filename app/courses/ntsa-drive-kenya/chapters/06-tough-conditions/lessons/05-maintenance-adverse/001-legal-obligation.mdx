Every technique from this chapter, dipped beams in fog, double following distance in rain, plant-and-steer braking, depends on the car actually being able to do what you ask of it. A worn wiper blade, a half-empty coolant tank, or a bald rear tyre cancels every good habit you have.

The Traffic Act puts this responsibility squarely on you. A vehicle on a Kenyan road must be in a roadworthy condition at all times, and the duty falls on the driver and the registered owner. If you drive it and it is not safe, that is your offence, not the mechanic's.

<Rule>
  The driver and owner are jointly responsible for ensuring the vehicle is in
  roadworthy condition before every journey, especially before a hard-conditions
  trip. "I didn't know it was broken" is not a legal defence.
</Rule>

This lesson is the bridge between weather technique and weather machinery. We sort common symptoms back to causes, open the bonnet for a five-point check, and lock in who is on the hook when it all goes wrong.

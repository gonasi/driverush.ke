  cards:
    - id: "card-1"
      front: "What is threshold braking?"
      back: "Threshold braking is the manual version of what ABS does automatically. You apply the brake hard enough to be just below the point where the wheels lock, hold it there, and release momentarily if they do lock. The 'threshold' is the boundary between maximum braking and a skid. You are doing the ABS module's job with your foot."
    - id: "card-2"
      front: "How do you find the threshold?"
      back: "Press the brake firmly but progressively. Feel the deceleration build. If the wheels lock, you will hear a tyre screech and the steering will go light. Release pressure just enough to get the wheels rolling again, then re-apply just below that pressure. This is the classic 'pump and release' technique for non-ABS cars: it is small, fast, controlled, not a full lift off."
    - id: "card-3"
      front: "How is it different from ABS plant-and-steer?"
      back: "On a non-ABS car you must not plant and steer. A locked front wheel cannot steer. So you keep braking just below lock-up, and only when you need to swerve, you ease pressure slightly to free the front wheels, swerve, then re-apply. Brake, ease, steer, re-brake. Sequential, not simultaneous."
    - id: "card-4"
      front: "How do you train the feel?"
      back: "Practice in a wide, empty space at low speed: a quiet morning car park or a clear murram track. Brake from 40 km/h to a stop, finding the firmest pressure that does not chirp the tyres. Do it ten times and your foot learns the threshold. It will save your life one day on the Mombasa Road."
  synthesis: "ABS car: plant and steer at the same time. Non-ABS car: brake just under lock-up, ease to swerve, brake again. Different vehicles, different feet. Know which one you are driving before you ever have to find out."

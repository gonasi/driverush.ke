You cannot carry a calculator. You need a single trick that works at any speed, on any road. It is called the **4-Second Rule**.

Pick a fixed landmark on the road ahead: a road sign, a tree, a painted line, a pothole. When the back of the car in front passes that landmark, start counting: _one Mombasa, two Mombasa, three Mombasa, four Mombasa_. If your front bumper reaches the landmark before you finish four, you are too close. Drop back until you can count cleanly.

<Mnemonic>
  4 seconds in the dry. 6 seconds in the rain. 10 seconds on ice or thick mud.
  Bigger gap when life gets harder.
</Mnemonic>

The rule is self-scaling. At 50 km/h, four seconds is roughly 56 metres. At 100 km/h it is 111 metres. You do not need to know the numbers, you just count to four.

<Callout type="tip">
  In NTSA theory questions about following distance, the answer is almost always
  the option that ties the gap to time (seconds), not metres. Time is what your
  brain and brakes actually deal in.
</Callout>

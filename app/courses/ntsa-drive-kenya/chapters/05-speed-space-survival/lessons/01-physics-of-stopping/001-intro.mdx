Here is the single fact that explains every fatal crash on Kenyan highways: **double your speed, and you quadruple your stopping distance.**

Not double. Quadruple.

<Callout type="analogy">
  Think of a stone dropped from a balcony. A stone falling from the second floor
  hits with a thud. The same stone from the fourth floor cracks the tile. Twice
  the height, four times the energy. Your car obeys the same physics: kinetic
  energy grows with the square of speed.
</Callout>

At 40 km/h you might stop in about 17 metres. At 80 km/h, you do not stop in 34. You stop in roughly 53 metres. Step up to 100 km/h and you need over 75 metres, the length of a football pitch from goal line to halfway.

<Rule>
  Stopping distance grows with the square of speed. Plan your buffer for the
  speed you are doing, not the one you wish you were doing.
</Rule>

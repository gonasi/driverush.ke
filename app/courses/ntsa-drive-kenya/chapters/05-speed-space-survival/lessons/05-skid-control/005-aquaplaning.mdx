Aquaplaning deserves its own paragraph because it punishes intuition harder than any other skid.

Your foot wants to brake. Your hands want to steer to the shoulder. Both inputs are useless until the tyre touches tarmac again, and then they are catastrophic, because all the saved-up input cashes in at once.

<Rule>
  Aquaplaning recovery: ease off the accelerator, hold the wheel straight, no
  brake, no steering input. Wait for the tyre to grip again. Then drive on
  gently.
</Rule>

<Callout type="tip">
  The single best aquaplaning defence is prevention: tread above 3 mm (not the
  legal 1.6 mm minimum, three), correct pressures, and dropping at least 10 km/h
  below your dry-road speed the moment the rain starts. The first 10 minutes of
  rain mix oil with water and are the deadliest stretch of any Kenyan storm.
</Callout>

You now have a clean kit for the physics-and-failure side of driving. The final lesson zooms out one more level: the mindset that prevents most emergencies before they happen, defensive driving.
